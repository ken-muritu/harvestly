How the countdown works.

One test, one forecast and two published tables give each batch of maize a number of storage days. Here is every step, with the numbers.

Try it on your own sacksJump to the tables

01 A day in the engine

Every evening at 17:00, five steps.

  1. 1. Starting moisture

    It comes only from the farmer’s own test. Salt sticks: above 15%, and the countdown uses 18%. Salt flows: below about 15%, and it uses 14.5%, never as proof of 13.5%. A hygrometer reading is converted with the moisture table at the reading’s temperature. A meter reading is used as read. Untested grain gets a request to test, never a countdown.

  2. 2. Drift

    Grain in woven sacks, cribs and floors takes on the moisture of the air around it. Each day it closes 5% of the gap to the moisture it would settle at in that air. Hermetic bags do not change. Drying grain is held until the next test.

    Example. Kitale in a normal October, 18.3 °C and 63% humidity 5, settles maize near 14.1% 6. A sack at 18% moves to 17.81% after one day.

  3. 3. Storage life used

    Each day uses 1 divided by the storage days at that temperature and moisture 7. When the fractions add up to 1, the grain has lost about 0.5% of its dry weight, which the source puts at roughly one US grade level. The source counts from harvest; the engine counts from the first test.

    Example. At 17.81% and 18.3 °C the table gives about 48 days, so that day uses 2.1% of the batch’s storage life.

  4. 4. Countdown and level

    The engine runs the days forward over the 7-day forecast, then repeats the forecast’s average, and counts the days until the life is used up. HIGH: 80% used, or 14 days or fewer. MEDIUM: 50% used, or 30 days or fewer. These thresholds are ours, set for the lead time sun-drying needs in a wet season.

  5. 5. What is sent

    At most one unprompted message a day, in this order: rain in the next 24 hours while the farmer is drying; a changed recommendation; a worse countdown (risk up, or days left halved and 60 or fewer); a spaced and capped reminder; the one-off insect reminder. A reply is answered at once and never uses up the evening slot.

    The repeat rules

    An ordinary repeat is spaced at least 3 days apart and sent at most twice per test. Re-tests are asked at most twice in 30 days, and rain alerts pause after three go unanswered.

    Urgent warnings (HIGH) repeat weekly, at most four times per test result. The fourth says it is the last reminder on that result and asks for a new test; after it the engine stays quiet until a new test result restarts the count, so a batch can go silent while its countdown is at zero.

02 Drift

Sixty days in a sack.

The same rule, run forward. Wet grain in dry air dries slowly. Dry grain in damp air does not stay dry: at 85% humidity sack B heads for 18.6% and passes the 13.5% line within days. That is the failure Harvestly watches for.

Also in the code
  • HORIZON_DAYS = 365. A countdown is capped at 365 days. The site shows 365+.
  • MAX_AST_DAYS = 3650. Storage time is capped at 3,650 days.
  • Forecast beyond day 7. After the 7-day forecast the engine repeats the forecast’s average.
  • Humidity clamp. Humidity above 90% is treated as 90%, and below 25% as 25%, because the moisture table ends there.
  • Same drift for all permeable storage. Sacks, cribs and floors drift at the same rate.
  • PLACE_MAX_CHARS. A place or stockist name is cut to a fixed length so a message stays within two SMS.
What the demos assume
  • CALIBRATION Salt that sticks is treated as 18% moisture (the true value is anything above 15%).
  • CALIBRATION Salt that flows is treated as 14.5% and never counts as proof of 13.5% or less.
  • CALIBRATION A woven sack closes 5% of the gap to the air's equilibrium moisture per day. The headline demo (261 days, then 34) depends on it: with 0%/day it reads 45, 43, 42 days; with 2%/day 145, 143, 38.
  • CALIBRATION Grain temperature equals the daily mean air temperature (the storage-time table is for grain temperature).
  • PROGRAMME Alert thresholds (80% / 50% of life, 14 / 30 days), spacing and caps are design rules, not findings.
  • UNVALIDATED The Arkansas tables were built for US corn and have not been validated for Kenyan white maize.
  • UNVALIDATED Outdoor regional weather stands in for the conditions inside the sack.
  • UNVALIDATED Daily means of temperature and humidity are used although the moisture table is not linear.
  • UNVALIDATED The storage-life clock starts at the farmer's first test, not at harvest (the source clocks from harvest).
  • UNVALIDATED Drying grain keeps its tested moisture and still uses up storage life at that moisture.
  • UNVALIDATED Hermetic bags are perfectly sealed: moisture stays constant.
  • UNVALIDATED Weather beyond the 7-day forecast equals the 7-day mean.
  • PROGRAMME A countdown is capped at 365 days (HORIZON_DAYS) and storage time at 3650 days (MAX_AST_DAYS); a 365 means 365 or more.
  • UNVALIDATED Sacks, cribs and floors all drift at the same rate.
  • UNVALIDATED Relative humidity above 90% is treated as 90% (the table ends there), and below 25% as 25%.

06 How it is checked

How it is checked.

The Python reference has automated tests. Its results are published as vectors.json: moisture values, storage-time values and day-by-day scenarios that any other version must reproduce. The JavaScript behind the calculator and the week replay on this site is checked against them by tools/test-engine-js.mjs, and a script asserts the replay equals vectors.json before the data is written.

What is not validated, and the pilot status, are listed once on the home page.
